Generate exactly three plausible but insufficient answer snippets for every supplied rubric
criterion. Each snippet must be short enough to isolate one failure mode while still sounding
like a realistic legal answer.

Required mutant types:

1. `bare_result`: mentions the topic or states the expected result but omits a rule,
   application, or controlling reason required by the criterion.
2. `wrong_scope`: uses a proposition about the wrong person, claim, offence, provision, or
   examination station without an explicit valid cross-reference. If the criterion cannot
   sensibly have a wrong-scope mutation, use the correct object at the wrong legal station.
3. `material_error`: contains a materially wrong legal rule, wrong competing provision,
   contradicted result, or invented/contradicted fact that should make the criterion fail.

Rules:

- The snippet must fail the original criterion for the stated reason; do not make it nonsense.
- Test the literal PASS components of the supplied criterion, not an additional detail found only
  in the source solution. A different person, victim, object, provision, or station is a valid
  wrong scope only when the criterion itself expressly requires the original scope.
- For `wrong_scope`, derive the required original person, object, provision, or station only from
  the criterion's `title` and `match_criteria`. Do not use a more specific provision or station
  merely because it appears in the human solution or task bundle. If the proposed
  `why_should_fail` depends on a legal citation or scope detail absent from both fields, the mutant
  is invalid and must be rewritten or marked not applicable.
- For an abstract rule-statement criterion that does not require a case-specific person or object,
  do not manufacture a wrong victim from the facts. Instead place the proposition expressly at an
  incompatible legal station and omit it at the station required by the criterion. A different
  station is incompatible only if the criterion makes the association material or the governing
  rule differs there. If the same abstract rule is legally transferable and the criterion accepts
  placement-flexible equivalent reasoning, mark `wrong_scope` not applicable instead of treating
  the location alone as an error.
- Before returning each mutant, identify in `why_should_fail` the exact mandatory phrase or
  component of the supplied criterion that the snippet violates. If you cannot identify one,
  rewrite the mutant. If that mutant type inherently cannot violate the literal criterion
  (for example, `bare_result` for a conclusion-only criterion or `wrong_scope` for a wholly
  abstract proposition without a required scope), return `"applicable": false`, an empty
  `answer`, and explain why no valid mutation of that type exists. Never fabricate an extra
  rubric requirement merely to keep all three types applicable.
- Preserve the case language.
- Do not copy or refer to professor, reviewer, or external-gold comments.
- Do not include several independent failure modes in one snippet.
- Do not omit the whole topic. The test is whether a superficially relevant but deficient
  answer is rejected.
- `why_should_fail` must identify the missing or wrong component in one sentence.
