## Style verdict
- Do not let the content verdict determine the style verdict. The legal rule, application,
  or conclusion may be substantively wrong and still be methodically well formed.
- The same person, legal object, issue, and examination station are hard requirements.
  Do not assemble a missing examination from statements scattered elsewhere.
- Pass only when the answer itself connects (1) a criterion-specific legal standard or
  decisive premise, (2) concrete facts or competing arguments, and (3) a conclusion that
  completes this exact line of examination.
- Routine points may be concise, but rule, fact, and result merely appearing near one
  another is insufficient without an expressed inferential connection.
- A standard for a neighboring issue is not criterion-specific. A complicity standard

- A later voluntariness discussion that implies continued ability does not constitute a

- Do not supply a missing premise, causal link, comparison, or conclusion. Irrelevant or

## Content verdict
- The criterion text is the only substantive standard. Apply its PASS and FAIL conditions
  and do not add requirements it does not state.
- Work in two steps. First locate the passage where the answer examines the criterion's
  own question: the same person, object, claim or offence, the same provision or
  fundamental right, and the same examination station. Then decide on that passage.
- Take the result and its reasons from that passage. Do not combine a result stated in
  one place with reasons given elsewhere for a different provision, right, claim, person,
  or station, even if the same doctrinal term appears there (the same term, such as an aim, an intent, or a duty, discussed for another right, offence, or claim). If the
  answer does not examine the criterion's question anywhere, the criterion is not met;
  passages on other questions neither prove nor disprove it.
- Accept equivalent legal wording, synonyms, and a valid alternative structure unless the
  criterion requires a particular form or location. Exact wording rarely matters.
- A PASS component is met when the answer states it expressly or when the answer's
  explanations on this question make it clear, for example because the answer establishes
  something stronger that necessarily includes it (an actual effect that the answer establishes includes the mere possibility of that effect). It is not met when the answer never takes a step the criterion
  requires, such as a prerequisite or the exclusion of an alternative, even if the
  answer's result is consistent with that step.
- A FAIL condition describing a legal position applies only when the answer adopts that
  position as its rule.
- A named fact-pattern variant, hypothetical, or subpart (for example, the base case
  versus a modification) is part of scope. If the answer expressly assigns its reasoning
  to a different named variant than the criterion, set `same_scope` to false and return
  content `fail`, unless the answer explicitly and unambiguously cross-applies that
  reasoning to the required variant. Do not fail solely because the answer omits the
  variant label when its surrounding context still unambiguously addresses the required
  variant.
- Facts or intent discussed for one offence or station do not establish the subjective
  element of a distinct preparatory offence or other station unless the answer expressly
  assigns them there or makes an unambiguous cross-reference.
- A heading, issue statement, bare result, or nearby mention does not prove that a required
  rule, application, or argument was performed.
- Evaluate the reason actually given. A materially wrong provision, doctrine, station,
  person, object, or invented material fact fails when incompatible with the criterion.
- When the criterion asks for a reason ("weil", "because"), a correct result at the
  correct place with a tenable, shorter reason suffices; it need not repeat the
  criterion's reason. Naming only the result or the statutory label is not a reason.
- Split the PASS side into mandatory components. Return pass when each is met expressly
  or clearly from the answer's explanations and no FAIL condition applies. Return fail
  when a component is missing, materially wrong, examined only under a different
  question, or only possibly intended.
