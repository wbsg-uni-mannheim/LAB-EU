## How to decide
- The criterion text is the only standard. Do not add requirements it does not state, and do not excuse a missing requirement because the rest of the answer is strong.
- Work in two steps. First locate the passage where the answer examines the criterion's own question: the same person, object, claim or offence, the same provision or fundamental right, and the same examination station. Then decide on that passage.
- Take the result and its reasons from that passage. Do not combine a result stated in one place with reasons given elsewhere for a different provision, right, claim, person, or station, even if the same doctrinal term appears there (the same term, such as an aim, an intent, or a duty, discussed for another right, offence, or claim). If the answer does not examine the criterion's question anywhere, the criterion is not met; passages on other questions neither prove nor disprove it.
- A PASS component is met when the answer states it expressly or when the answer's explanations on this question make it clear, for example because the answer establishes something stronger that necessarily includes it (an actual effect that the answer establishes includes the mere possibility of that effect). It is not met when the answer never takes a step the criterion requires, such as a prerequisite or the exclusion of an alternative, even if the answer's result is consistent with that step.
- A FAIL condition describing a legal position applies only when the answer adopts that position as its rule.
- Accept equivalent legal wording, synonyms, and a valid alternative structure unless the criterion requires a particular form or location. Exact wording rarely matters. Decide by legal meaning, not by keyword overlap.
- Never transfer reasoning about one person or legal object to another.
- Treat the examination station as part of the legal issue. A fact or rule used for one offence, claim, person, statutory provision, or procedural stage does not establish that the answer performed a different required examination. Cross-references pass only when the answer itself makes the reference and its scope unambiguous.
- A named fact-pattern variant, hypothetical, or subpart (for example, the base case versus a modification) is part of scope. If the answer expressly assigns its reasoning to a different named variant than the criterion, set `same_scope` to false and return `fail`, unless the answer explicitly and unambiguously cross-applies that reasoning to the required variant. Do not fail solely because the answer omits the variant label when its surrounding context still unambiguously addresses the required variant.
- A bare mention, heading, issue statement, or result does not prove that a required rule, examination, application, or argument was performed. If the criterion requires such legal work, the evidence must show that work in substance. Routine points may be brief, but they must still contain the proposition the criterion actually asks for.
- When the criterion requires application, the quoted evidence must itself connect a concrete case fact to the governing rule. A nearby rule statement plus a nearby result does not pass unless the answer makes that inferential connection.
- Evaluate the reason the answer actually gives. If it expressly derives the requested conclusion from a materially wrong provision, doctrine, legal station, person, or object, return "fail"; do not replace that stated reason with a legally correct inference that could have been drawn from the same facts.
- When the criterion asks for a reason ("weil", "because"), a correct result at the correct place with a tenable, shorter reason suffices; it need not repeat the criterion's reason. Naming only the result or the statutory label is not a reason.
- When source documents are supplied, reject evidence that depends on a material fact absent from or contradicted by those documents. Do not reward an invented fact merely because it would make the legal argument plausible.
- If the criterion names acceptable alternative positions, an answer taking one of them passes only if it also contains what the criterion requires for that alternative.
- First collect evidence: quote the shortest verbatim passages of the answer that bear on the criterion. Then reason from that evidence, then decide.
- Before returning "pass", perform the structured checks below. Split the PASS side into its mandatory components; identify the person/object and legal issue/station of both criterion and evidence; and assess the legal compatibility of the reason the answer actually states. Do not mark a component satisfied merely because the same sentence contains useful facts.
